# Essential Guitar Techniques

You now have the whole fretboard mapped — chords, pentatonic boxes, scale
positions. This lesson is about the other half of guitar playing: not *which*
note, but **how the note speaks**. These articulation techniques are what give
a guitar line its voice.

## Articulation — how a note begins and lives

Every note on a guitar can simply be picked. But picking every note makes a
line sound stiff and even. **Articulation techniques** change how a note is
started, connected and shaped — and that is where feel comes from. Five
techniques do most of the work.

## Hammer-ons and pull-offs

A **hammer-on** sounds a higher note *without picking it*: you "hammer" a
fretting finger firmly onto the string, and the impact alone makes it ring.

<PlayButton notes="C4 E4" label="A hammer-on — the second note is not picked" />

A **pull-off** is the reverse. From a fretted note, you "pull" the finger off
the string sideways, which plucks it slightly and sounds a **lower** note
already fretted beneath.

<PlayButton notes="E4 C4" label="A pull-off — sounding the lower note" />

Used together, hammer-ons and pull-offs let you play long runs with very little
picking. That smooth, flowing, barely-picked sound has its own name:
**legato**.

## Slides

A **slide** connects two notes by keeping a finger pressed down and **sliding**
it along the string from one fret to another. The pitch travels continuously
between the two notes instead of jumping.

A slide is also the cleanest way to perform the **position shift** from the
last lesson — slide into the new region on a single note and the hand arrives
without any audible break.

## Bends — the guitar's singing voice

A **bend** raises a note's pitch by **pushing or pulling the string sideways**
across the fret, stretching it tighter. The more you bend, the higher the
pitch climbs.

- A **half-step bend** raises the note by one semitone.
- A **full-step bend** raises it by a whole tone.

Bending is the guitar's most vocal technique — it is how the instrument
*sings* and *cries*. Recall the **blue note** from the Scales track: the true
blue note lives in the cracks between the frets, and a bend is exactly how a
guitarist reaches it — sliding the pitch up into that expressive, in-between
zone.

## Vibrato

**Vibrato** is a small, rapid, repeated bending of a held note — wobbling the
pitch slightly up and down. It does two things: it adds **sustain**, helping
the note ring longer, and it adds **expression**, giving the note a warm,
living quality instead of a flat, dead tone.

Vibrato is often what makes one player instantly recognisable. The notes can be
identical to another guitarist's; the *vibrato* is the fingerprint.

## The techniques at a glance

| Technique | What it does | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hammer-on | sounds a higher note without picking | smooth, legato |
| Pull-off | sounds a lower note without picking | smooth, legato |
| Slide | connects two frets with one finger | continuous, vocal |
| Bend | stretches the string to raise pitch | singing, crying |
| Vibrato | rapid small bends on a held note | sustain, expression |

## Why it matters

Scales and chords tell you the *notes*. These techniques decide whether those
notes sound mechanical or alive. A simple pentatonic lick played with confident
bends and vibrato beats a flurry of perfectly correct picked notes every time —
because music is not only the right notes, it is how they are *delivered*.

With the CAGED system, pentatonic boxes, position playing and now the essential
techniques, you have a complete foundation for the fretboard — and a complete
journey, from the names of seven notes all the way to expressive, professional
playing.
