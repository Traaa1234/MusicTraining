# Pentatonic Scales

The major and minor scales have seven notes. The **pentatonic** scales have
only **five** — *penta* is Greek for five — and dropping those two notes is one
of the most useful tricks in music. Pentatonics are the backbone of folk, rock,
blues, country and pop melody.

## Major pentatonic — the major scale, trimmed

The **major pentatonic** is simply the major scale with two notes removed: the
**4th** and the **7th**.

In C that means dropping F and B, leaving:

`C – D – E – G – A`

<Piano scale="C major-pentatonic" />

<PlayButton notes="C4 D4 E4 G4 A4 C5" label="Hear C major pentatonic" />

Why those two notes? The 4th and the 7th are the restless degrees — the 7th is
the leading tone, and the 4th leans toward the 3rd. They also form the
**tritone**, the most unstable interval. Remove them and what is left has no
half steps and no tritone at all. Nothing clashes.

## Minor pentatonic — the minor scale, trimmed

The **minor pentatonic** does the same trimming to the natural minor scale,
this time dropping the **2nd** and the **6th**.

In A minor that means dropping B and F, leaving:

`A – C – D – E – G`

<PlayButton notes="A3 C4 D4 E4 G4 A4" label="Hear A minor pentatonic" />

This is the single most-used scale in rock and blues lead guitar. Its shape on
the fretboard is the first "box" most guitarists ever learn:

<Fretboard scale="A minor-pentatonic" />

## They are two views of one scale

Look closely: **C major pentatonic** is `C D E G A`, and **A minor pentatonic**
is `A C D E G`. The same five notes — just started from a different home.

This is the **relative** relationship you will meet again in Key Signatures:
every major pentatonic *is* a minor pentatonic three half steps lower, and vice
versa. One set of five notes, two tonics, two moods.

| Scale | Notes | Built from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C major pentatonic | C D E G A | major scale minus 4 and 7 |
| A minor pentatonic | A C D E G | natural minor minus 2 and 6 |

## Why it sounds "safe"

Because there are no half steps between any neighbouring notes, there is no
strong dissonance to fall into. You can play the five notes in almost any order
over a matching chord and it will sound musical. That is why pentatonics are
the first scale handed to improvising guitarists — they let you make melodies
immediately, before you understand every rule.

The flip side: that safety can sound bland if you *only* ever use five notes.
Pentatonics are a confident starting point, not a finish line — the two notes
you removed are where colour and tension live.

## Why it matters

Pentatonic scales are everywhere: bagpipe tunes, gospel, blues licks, the black
keys of the piano (which form an F♯ major pentatonic all on their own). Learn
the two shapes and you can pick out a huge share of folk and popular melody by
ear — and you are one note away from the blues scale, which is next.
