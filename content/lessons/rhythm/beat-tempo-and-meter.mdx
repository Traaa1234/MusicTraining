# Beat, Tempo & Meter

Every lesson so far has been about **pitch** — which notes. This track is about
the other half of music: **rhythm** — *when* the notes happen. And rhythm
starts with the most basic thing of all, the steady pulse underneath the music.

## The beat

The **beat** is the regular pulse you feel in music — the thing you tap your
foot to, clap along with, or nod your head to without being told. It is steady
and even, like a clock ticking.

<PlayButton notes="C4 C4 C4 C4" label="Hear four steady beats" />

Those four clicks are evenly spaced. That evenness is the beat. Almost all
music is built over a pulse like this, even when the melody on top is doing
something far more complicated.

## Tempo — how fast the beat goes

**Tempo** is the *speed* of the beat. It is measured in **beats per minute**, or
**BPM** — literally, how many beats pass in sixty seconds.

| Tempo | BPM | Feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slow (a ballad) | 60–76 | calm, spacious |
| Medium (a pop song) | 90–120 | comfortable, walking pace |
| Fast (an upbeat dance track) | 130–160 | driving, energetic |

A tempo of 120 BPM means two beats every second. The higher the number, the
faster the pulse — but notice the *beat itself does not change*, only how
quickly it repeats.

## Meter — strong beats and weak beats

Listen to a pulse for a while and your ear does something automatically: it
**groups** the beats. The clicks stop sounding all equal — some feel *strong*
and some feel *weak*, in a repeating pattern.

That grouping is called **meter**. The two most common groupings are:

- **Duple** — beats group in **twos**: **STRONG**–weak, **STRONG**–weak. This
  is the feel of a march.
- **Triple** — beats group in **threes**: **STRONG**–weak–weak. This is the
  feel of a waltz.

Count along with almost any song — "1-2-1-2" or "1-2-3-1-2-3" — and you are
hearing its meter.

## The downbeat

The strong beat that begins each group has a name: the **downbeat**. It is
beat 1, the anchor of the group, the moment that feels like a fresh start.

The downbeat matters because so much of music is organised around it.
Melodies tend to land on it, chords tend to change on it, and a listener
unconsciously uses it to keep their place. Lose the downbeat and music feels
unmoored; feel it clearly and everything else lines up.

## Why it matters

Beat, tempo and meter are the invisible grid that all rhythm sits on. The beat
is the pulse, tempo is its speed, meter is how the pulse groups into strong and
weak. With that grid in place, the next lesson can put notes of different
**lengths** onto it — because rhythm is really about how long each note holds.
