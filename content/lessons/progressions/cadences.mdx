# Cadences — How Music Breathes

A **chord progression** is a sequence of chords moving through time. What makes
a progression feel like *music* rather than a list is the way it builds tension
and then releases it. A **cadence** is the moment of release — the chord move
that ends a musical phrase, the way a punctuation mark ends a sentence.

## Tension and home

Every progression you have harmonised carries a sense of **home** — the tonic,
the I chord — and a sense of being **away** from it. Move away and tension
rises; return and it resolves. A cadence is simply the standard way music makes
that return (or refuses to).

There are four cadences worth knowing. They are the full punctuation set of
tonal music: a full stop, a soft full stop, a comma, and a twist ending.

## The authentic cadence — the full stop

The strongest ending in music is **V → I** — the dominant chord resolving to
the tonic. This is the **authentic cadence**.

<PlayButton notes="G3 B3 D4" label="V — G major (tension)" chord />
<PlayButton notes="C4 E4 G4" label="I — C major (home)" chord />

It works because the V chord is packed with pull toward I — its leading tone
strains up a half step into the tonic. When V resolves to I with both chords in
root position, it is called a **perfect authentic cadence**, and it sounds
completely finished. It is how the vast majority of pieces end.

## The plagal cadence — the soft full stop

**IV → I** is the **plagal cadence** — gentler, with none of the sharp pull of
the dominant.

<PlayButton notes="F3 A3 C4" label="IV — F major" chord />
<PlayButton notes="C4 E4 G4" label="I — C major (home)" chord />

You know it as the **"Amen"** sung at the end of hymns. It resolves, but
softly — a sigh of arrival rather than a decisive landing.

## The half cadence — the comma

A **half cadence** does the opposite of resolving: it *ends on V*. The phrase
stops on the tension chord, leaving the ear hanging, certain that more is
coming.

<PlayButton notes="F3 A3 C4" label="IV" chord />
<PlayButton notes="G3 B3 D4" label="...stopping on V — unfinished" chord />

A half cadence is a comma, not a full stop. It is how the first half of a
melody pauses before the second half answers it.

## The deceptive cadence — the twist

The **deceptive cadence** sets up the strongest ending — V, the dominant — and
then, instead of landing on I, lands on **vi**.

<PlayButton notes="G3 B3 D4" label="V — promising a resolution to I..." chord />
<PlayButton notes="A3 C4 E4" label="...but landing on vi instead" chord />

Your ear was braced for home and got the relative minor instead. The effect is
a surprise — a way to extend a phrase, or to colour a moment with sudden
wistfulness, before eventually resolving for real.

## The four cadences at a glance

| Cadence | Chord move | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authentic | V → I | full stop — decisive ending |
| Plagal | IV → I | soft full stop — the "Amen" |
| Half | ends on V | comma — unfinished, expectant |
| Deceptive | V → vi | twist — expected I, got vi |

## Why it matters

Cadences are the punctuation of harmony. They are why a progression sounds like
a sentence with a shape rather than a string of chords. Hear which cadence ends
a phrase and you can feel exactly how "finished" the music intends to be — and
you are ready for the next lesson, which zooms in on the single most important
approach *to* the authentic cadence: the **ii–V–I**.
