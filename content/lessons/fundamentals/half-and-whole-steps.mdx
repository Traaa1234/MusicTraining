# Half Steps & Whole Steps

Every melody, scale, and chord you will ever play is built from just two
units of distance: the **half step** and the **whole step**. Get these and
the rest of theory stops being memorisation.

## The half step

A **half step** (or *semitone*) is the smallest distance in Western music —
the move from any key to the one right next to it, with nothing in between.
On a piano that is one key to the very next, black or white.

<PlayButton notes="C4 C#4" label="Hear a half step" />

C to C♯ is a half step. So is **E to F** — there is no black key between them,
yet it is still that same smallest distance.

## The whole step

A **whole step** (or *tone*) is simply two half steps stacked together. C to D
skips over C♯, so it covers two semitones.

<PlayButton notes="C4 D4" label="Hear a whole step" />

## Building the major scale

Here is the payoff. The major scale is nothing more than a fixed pattern of
whole (W) and half (H) steps:

`W – W – H – W – W – W – H`

Start on C, follow that pattern, and you land exactly on the white keys:

<Piano scale="C major" />

| Move | Distance | Lands on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C → D | Whole | D |
| D → E | Whole | E |
| E → F | Half | F |
| F → G | Whole | G |

The two half steps — between degrees 3–4 and 7–8 — are what give the major
scale its bright, resolved sound. Shift them and you get a different scale
entirely.

## Why it matters

Once you can *hear* the difference between a half and a whole step, you can
spell any scale or chord from a formula instead of memorising it. It is the
single most useful idea for a beginner.
