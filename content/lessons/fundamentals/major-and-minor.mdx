# Major & Minor — Two Colours of Sound

Ask anyone to describe music and two words turn up fast: **happy** and
**sad**. Musicians have precise names for those two colours — **major** and
**minor** — and the difference between them is far smaller than you'd guess.

## Two scales, two moods

- **Major** sounds bright, open, and resolved — think nursery rhymes,
  anthems, and celebration.
- **Minor** sounds darker, heavier, more serious — think suspense, longing,
  and reflection.

Here is a C **major** scale and a C **natural minor** scale. They begin and
end on the same note, yet the mood flips completely:

<Piano scale="C major" />

<Piano scale="C natural-minor" />

Look closely and you'll see most of the notes are *shared*. Only a few keys
shift between the two — and that small handful carries the entire change of
mood.

## It comes down to the third

If you had to point at **one** note that decides major versus minor, it is
the **third** — the third note of the scale, counting up from the tonic.

- In a **major** scale, the third sits a **major third** above the tonic —
  four half steps.
- In a **minor** scale, the third sits a **minor third** above the tonic —
  three half steps.

One half step. That single half step is the whole border between bright and
dark.

## Hear it in a chord

Stack that third together with the fifth and you get a **chord** — and now
the mood is unmistakable.

<PlayButton notes="C4 E4 G4" label="C major chord" chord />
<PlayButton notes="C4 Eb4 G4" label="C minor chord" chord />

Both chords share the same outer notes — C on the bottom, G on top. It is the
**middle note**, E versus E♭, that does all of the emotional work.

## Train your ear for it

You do **not** need perfect pitch to tell major from minor — almost everyone
can, the moment they know what to listen for. The trick is to stop hearing
"a chord" as one blurry sound and instead listen straight to **the third**:
is that middle note lifted and bright, or settled and dark?

That one question will carry you a remarkably long way once you reach the
ear-training exercises.

## Why it matters

Major and minor are the two pillars that every other sound is measured
against. Modes, chord qualities, key signatures — nearly all of theory is
really answering "how does this differ from plain major or minor?" Hear the
third clearly and the rest of music has a reference point.
