# Lydian & Mixolydian — The Bright Modes

Three modes have a **major third** and so sound broadly "happy": Ionian,
Lydian and Mixolydian. Ionian is just the major scale. This lesson covers the
other two — and the cleanest way to learn them is as **the major scale with
one note moved**.

## Lydian — major with a raised 4th

**Lydian** is the major scale with its **4th degree raised** a half step. In C
that turns the F into an **F♯**:

`C – D – E – F♯ – G – A – B – C`

<Piano scale="C lydian" />

<PlayButton notes="C4 D4 E4 F#4 G4 A4 B4 C5" label="Hear C Lydian" />

That ♯4 is Lydian's **characteristic note**. Compare the plain 4th of the major
scale with Lydian's raised one:

<PlayButton notes="C4 F4" label="Major scale — the natural 4th" />
<PlayButton notes="C4 F#4" label="Lydian — the raised 4th" />

The raised 4th is a tritone above the tonic, and it gives Lydian a weightless,
floating, slightly magical quality. It is the **brightest** of all seven modes
— even brighter than the ordinary major scale. You hear it constantly in film
and television scores reaching for wonder or wide-open space — the opening of
*The Simpsons* theme is a famous example.

## Mixolydian — major with a lowered 7th

**Mixolydian** is the major scale with its **7th degree lowered** a half step.
In C that turns the B into a **B♭**:

`C – D – E – F – G – A – B♭ – C`

<Piano scale="C mixolydian" />

<PlayButton notes="C4 D4 E4 F4 G4 A4 Bb4 C5" label="Hear C Mixolydian" />

The ♭7 is Mixolydian's **characteristic note**. Remember the major scale's
leading tone — that 7th degree straining a half step *up* into the tonic?
Mixolydian flattens it, so that pull is gone:

<PlayButton notes="B4 C5" label="Major — the leading tone snaps home" />
<PlayButton notes="Bb4 C5" label="Mixolydian — a softer, relaxed return" />

Without the hard leading-tone pull, Mixolydian sounds major but **relaxed and
earthy** rather than formal. It is the everyday sound of rock, blues, funk and
Celtic folk — and it is the natural scale of the **dominant 7th chord**.

## The two side by side

Both modes are one tweak away from the major scale — one tweak up, one tweak
down:

| Mode | Change from major | From C | Mood |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lydian | 4th raised → ♯4 | C D E F♯ G A B | bright, floating, magical |
| Ionian (major) | — | C D E F G A B | bright, resolved, neutral |
| Mixolydian | 7th lowered → ♭7 | C D E F G A B♭ | bright, relaxed, bluesy |

Notice the spectrum: Lydian is brighter than major, and major is brighter than
Mixolydian. Raising a note lifts the mood; lowering one settles it. That single
idea will carry you through the darker modes next.

## Using them

Both modes love a **static backdrop** — one droning chord that never moves.
Hold a C major chord and play C Lydian over it, leaning on the F♯, and the mode
blooms. Hold a C7 chord and play C Mixolydian, leaning on the B♭, and you have
the sound of a thousand rock jams. Without that anchor the ear drifts back to
the parent major scale.
