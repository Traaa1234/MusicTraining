# Dorian & Phrygian — The Dark Modes

Four modes have a **minor third** and so sound broadly "sad" or serious:
Dorian, Aeolian, Phrygian and Locrian. Aeolian is just the natural minor
scale. This lesson covers the two that flank it — and the cleanest way to learn
them is as **the natural minor scale with one note moved**.

## Dorian — minor with a raised 6th

**Dorian** is the natural minor scale with its **6th degree raised** a half
step. In C that turns the A♭ into a natural **A**:

`C – D – E♭ – F – G – A – B♭ – C`

<Piano scale="C dorian" />

<PlayButton notes="C4 D4 Eb4 F4 G4 A4 Bb4 C5" label="Hear C Dorian" />

That natural 6th is Dorian's **characteristic note**. Compare natural minor's
lowered 6th with Dorian's raised one:

<PlayButton notes="Ab4 C5" label="Natural minor — the dark ♭6" />
<PlayButton notes="A4 C5" label="Dorian — the brighter ♮6" />

It is still a minor scale — the ♭3 sees to that — but the raised 6th lets a
shaft of light in. Dorian is the **brightest of the minor modes**: minor with
hope, minor that is not quite grieving. It is the sound of *Scarborough Fair*,
of countless Celtic tunes, of Santana's *Oye Como Va*, and the jazz standard
*So What*.

## Phrygian — minor with a lowered 2nd

**Phrygian** is the natural minor scale with its **2nd degree lowered** a half
step. In C that turns the D into a **D♭**:

`C – D♭ – E♭ – F – G – A♭ – B♭ – C`

<Piano scale="C phrygian" />

<PlayButton notes="C4 Db4 Eb4 F4 G4 Ab4 Bb4 C5" label="Hear C Phrygian" />

That lowered 2nd is Phrygian's **characteristic note** — and it is a powerful
one. It sits only a **half step** above the tonic, so the very second note of
the scale leans hard against home:

<PlayButton notes="C4 Db4" label="The Phrygian ♭2 — a half step of tension right above home" />

That cramped half step at the bottom gives Phrygian a dark, tense, exotic
edge. It is the sound of **flamenco** and Spanish guitar, and — pushed harder —
the backbone of a great deal of metal.

## The two side by side

Both modes are one tweak away from natural minor — one tweak up, one tweak
down:

| Mode | Change from natural minor | From C | Mood |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dorian | 6th raised → ♮6 | C D E♭ F G A B♭ | minor, but hopeful |
| Aeolian (natural minor) | — | C D E♭ F G A♭ B♭ | plain minor, sad |
| Phrygian | 2nd lowered → ♭2 | C D♭ E♭ F G A♭ B♭ | minor, dark, Spanish |

The same spectrum from the bright modes appears again: **raising** a note
(Dorian's 6th) lifts the mood; **lowering** one (Phrygian's 2nd) darkens it.
Dorian is brighter than natural minor; Phrygian is darker.

## Using them

Like the bright modes, these want a **static anchor** — one unchanging chord or
bass note holding the tonic in place. Vamp on a C minor chord and play C
Dorian, leaning on that natural A, and the hopeful colour appears. Vamp on C
minor and play C Phrygian, leaning on the D♭, and you get the brooding Spanish
sound. Drop the anchor and the ear slides back to the parent major scale.
