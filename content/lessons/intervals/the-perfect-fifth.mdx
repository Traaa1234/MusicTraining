# The Perfect Fifth

The **perfect fifth** is the most stable, consonant interval after the octave.
Once you can hear it, you will hear it everywhere.

## What it is

A perfect fifth spans **seven half steps**. From C, count up seven semitones
and you land on G.

<PlayButton notes="C4 G4" label="Hear a perfect fifth" />

It is called *perfect* because it sounds neither happy nor sad — just open and
solid. That stability comes from physics: the two notes vibrate in a clean
**3:2 frequency ratio**, the simplest ratio after the octave's 2:1.

## Where you have already heard it

- The opening leap of *Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star*
- The first two notes of the *Star Wars* main theme
- Every guitar **power chord** — a power chord is literally a root and a fifth

## The fifth builds the circle

Stack perfect fifths and you generate all twelve keys. That is exactly what the
circle of fifths is: each clockwise step is one perfect fifth up. Click around
it and listen.

<CircleOfFifths />

## Spotting it by ear

The perfect fifth has a bright, hollow ring. Compare it with its near
neighbour, the perfect fourth (five semitones):

<PlayButton notes="C4 F4" label="Perfect fourth" />
<PlayButton notes="C4 G4" label="Perfect fifth" />

The fourth feels like it still wants to move somewhere. The fifth feels
finished.
